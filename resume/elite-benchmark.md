# Resume — Elite Benchmark

What genuinely strong technical resumes consistently do (2025–2026). Labels + confidence inline. These are the patterns to emulate; fresher-realistic calibration is in [`fresher-benchmark.md`](fresher-benchmark.md).

## 1. Structure & density
- `[FACT, High]` Reverse-chronological, **one page** for <10 years; two pages only if every line adds measurable value. (beamjobs, cvmark, resumevera.)
- `[FACT, High]` Dedicated **Technical Skills** block placed high — it is parsed first by ATS and scanned first by recruiters. (resumevera, beamjobs.)
- `[FACT, High]` **4–6 bullets** per role, no walls of text. (careerscribeai, cvmark.)

## 2. Bullets = impact, not duties
- `[FACT, High]` Google's recommended pattern (XYZ): *"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]."* Equivalent shape: **Action verb + what you built + technology + measurable impact.** (beamjobs, careerscribeai.)
- `[FACT, High]` Example of the standard: *"Redesigned authentication service using OAuth 2.0 and Redis, reducing login latency from 800ms to 90ms for 2M+ daily users."* (careerscribeai.)
- `[INFER, High]` The metric is the differentiator — but it must be **real**. A fresher rarely has 2M-user latency numbers; the elite move is to quantify what genuinely exists (counts, classes, mechanisms, time), never invent scale.

## 3. ATS realities
- `[FACT, High]` ~75% of resumes are filtered by ATS before a human reads them; parsers substring-match, so **exact keyword phrasing matters**; single-column, standard headings, no tables/graphics/columns that break parsing. (resumevera, cvmark.)

## 4. Differentiation signals elite resumes show
- `[INFER, High]` A crisp **specialization line** at the top (not a generic title); **named tools** every one of which recurs in a bullet; evidence of **engineering trade-offs** ("non-blocking scheduler, zero delay()"); links (GitHub/live demo) that actually work.

## 5. What elite resumes avoid
- `[FACT, High]` Buzzword objectives ("passionate, results-driven"), skill-bar graphics/rating percentages, photos, multi-column layouts, unquantified duty lists, and keyword stuffing that a technical screen would expose. (resumevera, beamjobs.)
