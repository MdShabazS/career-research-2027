# LinkedIn — Elite Benchmark

What strong technical LinkedIn profiles do (2025–2026). Note (per `METHODOLOGY.md`): LinkedIn profiles are not directly scrapeable; this synthesizes LinkedIn's own guidance + reputable recruiter analyses, treated as `INFERENCE` unless a primary source is cited.

## 1. Headline (the highest-value field)
- `[FACT, High]` The headline appears in search results, connection requests, comments, messages — it is the most-seen text and a primary keyword field. 220 characters. (dev.to, scoremyresume, careerbldr.)
- `[FACT, High]` Elite pattern: **title + key technologies + a value proposition**, not the bare word "Software Engineer." (dev.to, resumeworded.)
- `[INFER, High]` Strong headlines read like: `Role · core tech · differentiator · (current affiliation)`.

## 2. About (2,600 chars, first person)
- `[FACT, High]` Open with a 1–2 sentence **hook** on what you do and for whom (current value, not career history); then technical depth (languages/frameworks/domains); then **2–3 achievements with real numbers**; close with a call to action. (resumeworded, careerbldr, elev-ai.)
- `[INFER, High]` Elite About sections sound human and specific, avoid "passionate/results-driven," and mirror the resume's positioning without copying it verbatim.

## 3. Featured, Experience, Skills
- `[FACT, High]` **Featured** section surfaces best proof (repo, portfolio, a project writeup). Keep Skills relevant and endorsed; Experience bullets carry a couple of quantified outcomes. (jobwizard, careerbldr.)
- `[FACT, Medium]` "Open to Work" set to **recruiters-only** increases relevant outreach without signaling to the whole network. (scoremyresume.)

## 4. Cross-channel consistency
- `[INFER, High]` Elite profiles are consistent with the resume, GitHub, and portfolio — same headline identity, same dates, same project names. Recruiters cross-reference; drift is a negative signal.

## 5. Credibility signals vs. noise
- `[INFER, High]` Signals: named tech in context, real metrics, live links, genuine recommendations, coherent narrative. Noise: skill dumps, buzzword summaries, "1.5 years experience" inflation, endorsements for skills never used.
